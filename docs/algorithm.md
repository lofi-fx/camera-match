# Matching model

Inputs are DaVinci Wide Gamut / DaVinci Intermediate. Channel decoding uses the published DaVinci Intermediate linear toe and log branch; RGB is then handled in scene-linear DWG. The DWG↔XYZ(D65) matrices are from [Blackmagic’s specification](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/InformationNotes/DaVinci_Resolve_17_Wide_Gamut_Intermediate.pdf) and embedded in `src/core/Color.cpp`. OKLab uses [Björn Ottosson's XYZ(D65) matrices](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/) with signed cube roots and no sRGB clipping. Output is encoded back to Intermediate. Negative and above-one channels are retained.

Each capture stores the per-patch robust RGB mean, dispersion, usable and candidate pixel counts, quality flags, chart geometry, source bounds, render scale, pixel aspect ratio, chart model, name, time, and revision. Hero and target observations are measured from the Source clip before this node's correction. The checksum-validated `CM2` payload contains both captures and the frozen solution. Rendering never fetches the chart or refits.

The solver takes the median log2 luminance ratio of usable neutral pairs as exposure stops. Relative neutral channel gains are estimated from the same pairs, with their common gain removed. Skin and chromatic pairs then fit first-harmonic periodic hue offsets and log relative-saturation gains in OKLab. The fit is regularized toward zero and requires four populated hue sectors; limited coverage retains neutral/exposure matching. Hue is capped at ±30°, log saturation at ±log(2), and the hue field derivative is limited to avoid folds. These are safety limits, not a guarantee of camera gamut matching.

At render, neutral correction is rescaled to preserve input linear Y. Hue and saturation are evaluated from the same original hue for that stage, converted back to DWG, and rescaled again to preserve Y. Exposure applies a uniform `2^(amount × fitted stops)` gain last. When Exposure Match is zero, positive stable pixels retain incoming linear Y within numeric tolerance. Very dark/negative or unstable pixels skip chromatic changes, and all-zero controls and Bypass take an exact copy path for matching source/output formats. No per-frame fitting occurs.

Relative saturation is defined as OKLab chroma divided by positive OKLab lightness, **not** HSV saturation. A single global exposure gain does not fix differing camera tone response. Later display transforms can change perceived brightness even if scene-linear Y is preserved here.
